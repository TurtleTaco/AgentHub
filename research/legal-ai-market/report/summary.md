# Legal AI market summary (2026-11-14)

- **696** companies profiled (572 core, 124 adjacent) in **47** countries
- **36** more absorbed/defunct companies tracked for M&A; **23** candidates excluded with reasons
- **15** workflow domains, **68** categories

## Key takeaways

1. Capital is concentrated. Harvey, Clio, Legora, Filevine and EvenUp hold 44% of the ~$11B disclosed to core legal AI companies.
2. Horizontal AI workspaces (Harvey, Legora and peers) take 23% of all disclosed funding, though only 52 companies are built mainly as workspaces.
3. Contracts is the most crowded domain. 193 companies touch it. Contract review is bundled by 131 but is the main focus of only 24.
4. Case-law research is now table stakes. 138 companies ship it, mostly inside broader assistants, drafting or litigation tools.
5. Most players are specialists. 70% cover three categories or fewer; only 20 companies span eight or more.
6. The post-ChatGPT wave is real. 36% of companies with a known founding year started in 2023 or later.
7. A services model is emerging. 76 companies sell legal outcomes, not software, including AI-native law firms like Crosby, Garfield.law and Lawhive.
8. Consolidation is speeding up. LexisNexis (7 deals), Clio (6), Legora (5) and Onit (5) lead; all five Legora deals closed in 2026.
9. White space: court AI (9 players), legislative drafting (8), criminal defense (6), arbitration (6) and legal-AI evaluation (5) are the thinnest categories.
10. In-house legal is the most targeted buyer (413 companies), ahead of mid-size firms (330) and large firms (263).
11. North America hosts 54% of companies, Europe 24%, Asia-Pacific 14%. Latin America is early, but Brazil's Enter already reached a ~$1.2B valuation.
